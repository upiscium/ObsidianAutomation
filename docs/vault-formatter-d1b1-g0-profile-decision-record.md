## The collision that must be resolved before profile adoption

- **Controlling [#298](https://github.com/upiscium/ObsidianAutomation/issues/298) (updated 2026-10-24):** UX-first fresh single-LXC Standard MVI with **normally one nonroot service user, one Nextcloud account and one WebUI**. Subsystem-specific Unix accounts, multiple Nextcloud accounts and formal high-assurance isolation are **not mandatory MVI criteria**. Nonroot, safe CAS, Off/Dry-run, privacy and explicit authorizations remain mandatory.
- **Existing open [#302](https://github.com/upiscium/ObsidianAutomation/issues/302) / [#299](https://github.com/upiscium/ObsidianAutomation/issues/299) terminology:** *Standard* is the one-Nextcloud-account connection approach; *Strict* is a proposed/legacy-compatible **multiple remotely scoped Nextcloud accounts with server-side ACLs**, not automatically an adopted fresh-MVI requirement.
- **Merged [#571](https://github.com/upiscium/ObsidianAutomation/pull/571) B5 document:** calls the D1/Owner/Broker three-UID mechanism an "optional Strict effect-execution profile" **as a proposal**. Here "Strict" means **local Unix process and credential/egress separation**, not the #302 remote account meaning. Those guarantees are independent. Keeping one unqualified `Strict` label is a semantic and acceptance-risk collision, not evidence of implementation or a resolved product decision.
- **#472 high-level acceptance:** does not universally mandate three distinct Unix UIDs. The proposed D1 mechanism specifically requires Worker, Owner, Broker separation for its **strong local enforcement**; B4 proves real kernel cross-UID/GID only in disposable namespaces, not on production hosts.

## Model two independent axes instead of one overloaded profile

| Remote Nextcloud credential policy (#302 axis) | Local process / effect-execution policy (D1 axis) | What may eventually be claimed, only after evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **One account** (Standard target) | **Single nonroot service UID** (Standard target) | One-LXC ease of setup. Software policy/CAS can protect against accidental conflicts. **No** claim of isolation from compromise of that UID; an exposed shared credential can carry its account's full remote rights. |
| **Multiple ACL-scoped accounts** (#302 Strict *remote* mode) | **Single nonroot UID** | Separate *remote* permissions can constrain a credential's server-side scope, but same-UID local application compromise may access credentials held by that UID. Not the D1 Broker isolation guarantee. |
| **One account** | **Isolated Broker execution** (candidate D1 mechanism: three UIDs) | Local Worker/Owner separation from Broker secret and Provider transport **only if** executable, IPC, credential and egress policy are actually enforced; compromise of the one remotely privileged Nextcloud account is **not** limited by multi-account remote ACLs. |
| **Multiple ACL-scoped accounts** | **Isolated Broker execution** | Both separate layers might reduce exposure, but each needs its own authorization and proof. Most operationally expensive; not an MVI prerequisite, not established by B4/PR #571. |

**Naming proposal (not yet adopted):** Reserve *Standard / Strict remote* for #302's **credential-account dimension**, and refer to the D1 local mechanism as **`Broker-isolated execution`** or **`D1 isolated-broker`**, not simply `Strict`. These are conceptual axes, **not** four supported installer modes or UI toggles. Do not advertise unsupported combinations, autoconfigure them or silently migrate between them.

Separate third axis: **Formatter effect mode** `Off / Dry-run / Auto apply` is neither a remote account profile nor an OS isolation profile. It remains **Off by default** and requires its own exact human/provider/Writer authorization for any live effect, irrespective of either of the above axes.

## Decision alternatives — Option A selected, B/C not selected

**A. Defer product adoption of Broker-isolated execution; retain source-only D1 research (HUMAN-APPROVED G0 DECISION).** Prioritize #298 Standard MVI for product delivery. Defer **only** adopting, provisioning and activating Broker-isolated execution as a supported product profile. This does **not** revoke or suspend separately approved #472/D1 source-only design, code, CI or disposable-fixture work within its original scope; it grants no new authorization for any work. Keep the D1/B0–B5 code/docs as reviewed, OFF/unwired research and optional future validation, with no requirement to create three OS users, install Broker units or add Nextcloud accounts for Standard. Do not pretend this establishes strong Broker authority. This avoids committing maintenance, installer upgrades, account/IPC recovery and E2E support to an unproven optional deployment mode. Keep the future execution axis available for reconsideration, not promised or hidden as a supported feature.

**B. Adopt Broker-isolated execution as an optional future *product requirement* now.** Explicitly accept additional installer profiles, three-user lifecycle/privilege management, per-UID egress/credential custody, audited FD and IPC setup, journaling/recovery, negative policy tests, documentation, support, upgrade/rollback and concurrency risk. The option is **not usable** until G1–G7 are individually proven and separately activated. This does not automatically require multi-account Nextcloud Strict. Requires separate owner decision and scoped implementation plan.

**C. Require Broker isolation for Standard MVI (REJECT).** Contradicts the accepted #298 fresh one-user goal and risks turning a complex, unwired security research component into a blocker for usable product delivery. Do not choose this without expressly changing #298 by a new human decision.

**Why defer is not a safety downgrade:** A does **not** authorize ordinary one-UID Auto apply or silently swap a strict effect session for a weaker Writer. It makes no new effect path executable. If a future operator explicitly requests a Broker-isolated operation and the necessary enforcement is absent, **deny that operation**. Any Standard Writer must independently pass its approved CAS/immutable-plan/ETag/readback/idempotence/Off/restart/privacy acceptance, with its *own explicit live-effect authorization*, and must disclose the one-UID/one-account compromise limits; B4 source-only PASS is not that approval.

## Threat model and gating before revisiting A→B

- **T1 data integrity, accidental dual writes, stale reads and unknown outcomes:** needs canonical target scope, strong ETag/If-Match, durable one-shot receipt or uncertainty quarantine, verified readback, Off semantics and no blind retry. Applies to Standard and any isolated executor; neither UID separation nor multiple accounts is a substitute.
- **T2 compromise of the nonroot Worker principal, with Owner and Broker remaining trusted:** demands real separate local identities, inherited-fd control, Broker-only secret custody, real kernel peer credentials **plus trusted executable provenance**, enforced egress restrictions, systemd/userns/ACL hardening, complete restart/lifecycle and negative testing. Compromise of the trusted Owner or credential-holding Broker is outside this narrower Worker-compromise assurance. Current B4 only covers a subset of IPC identity in ephemeral namespaces.
- **T3 stolen/reused remote service credential:** reduction of server-side blast radius demands real Nextcloud account ACL scoping and account rotation. Three local UIDs with one shared remotely broad account do not supply T3's remote protection.
- **T4 trusted host-root compromise / malicious Provider:** out of scope for the proposed nonroot isolation profile; do not claim protection.
- **B5 G1–G3 (host principals, IPC, executable identity) and G4–G7 (credentials, effect admission, provider finality, live canary)** stay separately unmet. Passive inventory, active synthetic IPC, lifecycle fault injection, provisioning and Provider tests each require distinct explicit authorization; never use the existing `obsidian-control` as disposable scratch without approval.
